Read this as evidence, not a promise. Backtests and paper trading show what past data and pretend money did. Real trading usually earns less, there are losing days and losing weeks, and nothing here is guaranteed.

The edge

How it makes money, in six steps

Each Kalshi market asks one question: will this coin finish the next 15 minutes at or above where it started? Kalshi's prices sometimes lag behind the real coin price. That lag is the whole idea.

    The evidence ladder

    Five tests, each harder to fool than the last

    The same strategy has to keep making money as the test gets more realistic: first on data it was tuned on, then on days it never saw, then on months it never saw, then with live prices, then with real orders on Kalshi's demo exchange.

            How this test works

            Your bet size, your limits, Kalshi's real trades

            Backtests usually assume every order fills at the price the bot wanted. Real orders don't always: someone has to be trading at that price at that moment. This test replays each strategy as if it ran on your account, and only counts a bet if Kalshi's own trade records show it would have filled.

            Results

            Which strategies hold up with real money

            Consistency score (t): how steady the profit was from one bet to the next, not just how big it was. Around 2 or more is unlikely to be luck; under 1 is mostly noise.
