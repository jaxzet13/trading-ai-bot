Backtested. No edge. Not betting parlays.

A parlay only pays if every leg wins, and then multiplies the legs' payouts. I built parlays from the same tennis bets the lab tests and scored them on 64 days of settled matches. Parlays multiply whatever the legs earn, and the legs earn almost nothing, so parlays mostly lose faster.

Why a parlay can't create an edge

A parlay is the same as betting leg 1 and putting every cent of the winnings on leg 2. If each leg earns 1%, two legs earn about 2% and three about 3%, with a much bigger chance of losing everything. If each leg loses 2% (the spread plus the fee), two legs lose about 4% and three about 6%. That is exactly what the backtest shows.

Picking the best parlay in advance

The few positive parlay results rest on 7 to 28 parlays. To check them fairly, each test day picked the leg pool and parlay size with the best record on earlier days only, then bet it.

Kalshi's own parlays ("combos")

Kalshi does sell parlays, and Challenger and ITF matches can be legs. But a combo market is only created when someone asks for a quote, and market makers price it on the spot. On Nov 26 there were 4 open combos with a tennis leg, none of them traded, and no price history at all, so they can't be backtested. Combo market makers usually charge more than the product of the leg prices, which would make them worse than building the parlay yourself.

What happens next

No parlays. The tennis auto-better keeps betting single Challenger favourites, 2 contracts each, with the −$10 stop. In this run those singles earned +0.65% a bet (t 0.5): still a small, unproven edge.
