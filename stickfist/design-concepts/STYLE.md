# The look to aim for in card art

Ten reference images the owner sent on 2026-11-25 with "remember these styles". They are references for a look, not pictures to
reproduce: new card art should be original work in this vein. (Four existing cards already come from this family: Orbit, Null,
Phantom and Shogun.)

## What they have in common

- **Dark.** Black or near-black ground, with the subject carved out of it by light. Most of the frame is shadow.
- **Grain you can see.** Film grain, photocopy noise, dither, halftone, coarse pixels, canvas weave. Nothing is smooth or clean.
- **High contrast, few colours.** Pure black and white, or one colour against black (cold blue, indigo, violet), sometimes one
  small warm accent. The exception is deliberate: an oil-slick rainbow on black.
- **A figure dwarfed or transformed.** A small lone silhouette in a huge space; an angel; a knight; a body turning into a
  galaxy, a star, a portal. Faces are hidden, turned away, helmeted or missing.
- **Mythic and melancholy, not cute.** Angels, knights, graveyards, cosmos, the uncanny. Quiet or solemn more often than violent.
- **Looks found, not rendered.** Like a scan of a print, a still from a damaged tape, a photo of a painting. No glossy
  3D-render or "AI concept art" sheen, no lens flares, no busy detail everywhere.

## The ten, as styles

1. **Painted pulp horror.** A caped figure in a wide hat in a moonlit graveyard, mist, a green spectral blast. Deep blue night,
   soft brushwork, 1980s paperback-cover feel.
2. **Oil-slick glitch.** A tiny silhouette on a plain under a warped, tunnelling sky; rainbow chromatic smears over grey and
   black, VHS grain, scan lines.
3. **Screenprint collage.** A featureless chrome head in stark cream-on-black posterisation; a hand reaches from a portal and
   the face smears toward it. Indigo star field of fine concentric lines. Halftone and paper texture.
4. **Pixelled cosmos.** A torso whose head is a blue-white galaxy with ringed planets orbiting it; the body breaks into coarse
   pixels. Blue on black, with a small sideways timestamp like a camcorder frame.
5. **Gouache on black.** A knight in plate armour holding a bunch of white daisies with yellow centres, white strokes of rain.
   Thick, visible, chalky brush marks; grey and white with one warm note.
6. **Emblem.** A four-pointed white star with a dark eye at its centre, gripped by rust-brown fingers over cracked glowing
   blue. Flat black ground, hard edges, reads as a sigil.
7. **Stippled statue.** A winged figure with a sword on a rock, almost lost in black, picked out in white grain like a
   long-exposure or a scratchboard.
8. **Photocopied engraving.** Two angels in clouds, one falling and one reaching down. Harsh two-tone, heavy speckle, the
   look of a 19th-century engraving run through a copier.
9. **Sand grain.** A blurred winged figure walking in a field of coarse white noise and black cloud. Almost abstract; the
   shape survives only as a silhouette.
10. **Blown-out duotone.** A kneeling knight with a sword planted in the grass and huge wings burned out to pure white. Deep
    blue and white, rain streaks, bloom, coarse print grain.

## Using it

- Ask for the medium and the damage as much as the subject: "grainy two-tone photocopy", "gouache on black paper",
  "dithered blue duotone", "VHS still with chromatic smear", "stippled white on black".
- One subject, lots of black, light from one source. Leave empty space.
- Keep the game's stick figure where it fits (a small lone figure for scale), but the higher the rarity, the more the picture
  should read like one of these.
- Good matches for the ASCII renderer: anything with a strong silhouette and large dark areas (2, 4, 6, 7, 10). Fine speckle
  (8, 9) turns to mush below about 200 characters across, so save those looks for the big cards.
